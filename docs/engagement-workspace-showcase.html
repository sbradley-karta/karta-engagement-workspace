What is new

Not another tool. A single place, per engagement, where the tools we already have meet a controlled use of AI. The team opens it, the week's evidence is already there, and every AI suggestion passes through a named person before it goes anywhere.

One place per engagement

Every engagement gets its own workspace, stood up from a blank template in about ten minutes by the engagement owner: client and project names, and links to the Asana project, the SharePoint folder, the Granola folder and the Harvest project. Nothing is copied in. The workspace reads the originals.

AI that works inside the rules

Claude drafts the week's status from what the sources say, only when someone clicks Draft, and only on that person's seat. Dates, status colors and the deck itself are produced by code, not by the model. A named reviewer corrects and approves before anything is built.

Built to repeat across projects

The same workspace, the same rules and the same deck template for every engagement. A written stand-up process, an onboarding guide, and a second workspace already in the hands of two colleagues for a cold test.

What happens on a Friday

Five steps. The consultant reads, corrects, approves and sends. Everything else is done for them.

1

Open

Open the engagement's workspace. Asana and SharePoint are read as you, so you see what you are allowed to see.

2

Review

Milestones, dates and last week's deck are already on screen, with anything missing called out.

3

Draft

One click. Claude writes the accomplishments, plans and risks and suggests a status for each area.

4

Approve

Fix what is wrong, then approve under your own name. The approved version is kept, with who and when.

5

Deck

The KCG status deck is built from the approved values and lands in the Status & Steer Co folder. You send it, as today.

How we keep it under control

These are the design, not the fine print. They are why this can be used on client work.

Reads as the person looking

No shared logins. If you are not on the project in Asana or SharePoint, the workspace shows you nothing from it.

A named person approves

Every value that reaches a client was approved by someone who put their name on it. The AI never approves.

Nothing is sent by software

The workspace produces the deck. A person opens it and sends it. That does not change.

Missing evidence is never Green

If a source is missing or a date is unknown, the status says so. It cannot quietly default to good news.

Client data stays put

Nothing is copied into a new system. The only new record is the approved status, kept inside Karta's Claude account.

What is coming

The next four weeks

  • Two engagements, weekly. Legend plus the workspace the cold-test colleagues stand up. Every Friday, a status approved and a deck built.
  • Measured, not guessed. The workspace records how long approval takes and how much of the draft was corrected. We will show you the trend, not an opinion.
  • Decks land in SharePoint on their own. One IT approval away. Until then the workspace hands the file to the reviewer to save.
  • Cold-test findings folded in. Whatever confused a colleague who had never seen it gets fixed before the next engagement.

After that, on the same journey

  • Sprint review preparation. What will be shown, which requirements it covers, what evidence exists.
  • Meeting capture. Workshop notes from Granola turned into actions and decisions the team can approve.
  • UAT preparation. Approved requirements turned into test scripts.
  • Onboarding brief. A new team member brought up to speed from the approved record, not from a folder hunt.

Each arrives the same way Weekly Status did: built once, proven on a real engagement, then turned on for the others.
